# Final Project Release + Presentation

For the final project release and presentation, you will have completed the development of your
website and fully deployed it to its target platform.
As discussed throughout the course, the goal is to create a great product that is usable and useful and
that you can be proud of. Your project should have all of the necessary use cases completed from the
Software Requirements Specification and be a fully functional product.
If your initial requirements did not fully take into account all that was needed to build your product
properly, you are expected to update your schedule and work plan appropriately to make the best
possible final product given your time constraints. Any changes that reduce the scope of your project
should be done only with instructor approval.
Your group will give a presentation in class, showing off the final product. The project is expected to be
in full working order and any audience member should be able to signup and begin using it during your
final presentation. Make sure that your homepage has instructions and information for newcomers to
your website to understand how to use it. The expectation is that a potential user who is unfamiliar with
your website should be able to understand and use your website on their own.
Be sure to test all of the requirements for your project and ensure they are working and handle common
error conditions. If there are any known issues, these should be noted and updated to the bug report
(though all of your core requirements are expected to be working).

## How to Submit

The final source code for your released project should be in the main (or master) branch of your group’s
GitHub repository. Your GitHub README file should be up to date with instructions for setting up the
project and building, testing, and deploying the project as described in the Milestones. The final report
document and PPT slides should be submitted to Brightspace.
If your project website has “admin” accounts or other special accounts that are necessary to see the full
functionality you developed, you should email the instructor the log in credentials of a sample user
account of this type.
In class you will give a presentation where you will demo the app as if the audience were a potential
customer — you are "pitching" the product to us. This presentation should be around 30 minutes.
After the presentation, everyone will individually complete a brief self reflection and peer assessment.
You should also submit the final report document in your GitHub and let the instructor know it.
Your website should remain hosted online until you receive your final project grade, which should
happen within a week of the submission.


## Grading (70% of your grade)

Your project itself is graded on:

- (^) Being a great product that is usable and useful.
- (^) Overall technical implementation: the expectation is that you will write quality code that has a
modular architecture that is easy to extend and evolve if you were to continue the project.
- (^) Your project’s code should follow the code conventions you decided to follow in your Software
Design Document.
- (^) Meeting the bar you set for yourself, and that we agreed on together was a reasonable amount of
work for a semester-long, capstone portfolio project.
Doing the bare minimum to satisfy all of the requirements will translate into a ‘C’ grade for the project.
In terms of grades for your final project, the expectations for each letter grade are below:
A: Superior, striking, or unexpected pieces of work with excellent effort demonstrating a mastery of
software engineering skills and a thoughtful use of concepts discussed in class; work that shows
imagination, clarity of presentation and documentation, originality, creativity, effort, and attention to
detail.
**B** : Good work demonstrating quality software engineering skills, with adequate preparation and clear
presentation and documentation.
**C** : Work that is adequate but that would benefit from increased effort or preparation.
**D** : Work that needs more effort.
To qualify for a ‘B’ or ‘A’ grade, it is expected that you go above and beyond the bare minimum to make
thoughtful features and a great overall product experience, both in terms of making the website
streamlined and easy to use, as well as supporting the full range of features you committed to, and
having a professional quality design.
As part of this turn-in, every member will do an individual self reflection and peer assessment. The
expectation is that all group members did roughly equal portions of the project. However, if that is not
the case, you may receive a different grade than your teammates.
